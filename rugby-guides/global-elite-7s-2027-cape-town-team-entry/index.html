Global Elite 7s 2027 in Cape Town: what teams need to know

Everything coaches, players and parents need to know about the u23 sevens tournament in Cape Town from 26 to 28 November 2027, from eligibility to registration.

What the Global Elite 7s is

The Global Elite 7s is an elite international u23 sevens tournament in Cape Town, South Africa, running from 26 to 28 November 2027. It brings together leading provincial, professional academy, university, club and invitational teams from around the world, and it sits outside the regular HSBC SVNS circuit. If you coach or manage a strong young sevens side, this is a chance to measure your players against unfamiliar opponents in a single, high-quality weekend.

The field is set at 48 teams: 24 men and 24 women, all in the u23 age group. The venue is still to be confirmed, so keep an eye on the event page for updates. The prize is a real incentive: the winner in Cape Town gets free team entry into the Mauritius series in 2028.

It is also a festival, not just a draw sheet. Alongside the rugby there is live entertainment, a beer garden and food vendors, a cultural experience, and a welcome and closing party. For families and supporters travelling with a team, that makes the weekend worth planning around.

Who can enter, and what eligibility means for your squad

Only top elite teams may register. The event is open to provincial, professional academy, university, club, invitational and international sides, and every team plays in the u23 age group. When you register, the organisers confirm eligibility and next steps, so treat your entry as an application rather than an automatic place.

Before you send anything, do some homework on your own squad:

  • Check every date of birth. Build a simple spreadsheet with each player, their birth date and their passport or ID number. Ask the organisers to confirm the exact age cut-off rather than assuming.
  • Be honest about your level. Note recent results, competitions played and any representative players in the group. A short, factual summary helps the eligibility conversation.
  • Pick a clear team category. The form asks whether you are a club, academy, university, invitational, provincial or international side. Choose the one that matches how your squad is actually selected.
  • Name one team manager. A single point of contact with a working WhatsApp number and email keeps communication clean.

Individual players without a squad can also register as a player on the same page and receive confirmation of eligibility and next steps.

The week in Cape Town for international teams

International teams get an 8 day, 7 night programme, from arrival on 22 November to departure on 29 November 2027. The shape of the week looks like this:

  • Arrival day: international teams land and settle in.
  • Next day: team run and excursions.
  • Midweek: player combine and welcome party.
  • The day before kick-off: another team run and excursions.
  • Three tournament days: the rugby runs from 26 to 28 November.
  • Final morning: breakfast, then departure.

Those lead-in days matter. A long-haul flight leaves legs heavy and sleep patterns scrambled, so use the first team run as a loosener rather than a hard session. Short, sharp handling drills, some light running and a stretch will do more good than a full contact session. Get players outside in daylight, keep them hydrated and stick to normal meal times to help body clocks adjust.

The player combine is a chance for individuals to show what they can do, so prepare players for it. Make sure they arrive rested, warmed up and in proper boots for the surface.

Preparing a squad for three days of sevens

Sevens across three days is a test of repeat speed and freshness as much as skill. Plan your build-up with that in mind.

  • Conditioning: train for repeated sprints with short rest, not long steady runs. Sevens rewards players who can make a second and third effort in the same passage.
  • Squad roles: settle your kick-off receivers, your restart strategy and your scrum trio well before you travel. In a tight game, restarts often decide who has the ball.
  • Between games: have a routine ready for the gaps between matches. Change into dry kit, refuel with simple carbohydrates and fluids, stay off your feet and keep warm if the wind picks up.
  • Sun and wind: Cape Town in late November can be bright and breezy. Pack sunscreen, hats and plenty of water bottles, and practise kicking and high-ball catching in windy conditions at home.
  • Kit spares: bring spare boots, studs, strapping tape and a second set of playing kit.

Think about game management too. Work out who calls the shots on the field, how you will use replacements across a long day, and what you will change if you go behind early.

Travel and logistics tips for touring teams

Good logistics take pressure off the coaching staff and let players focus on rugby.

  • Documents: check passports are valid well beyond your return date and look up visa requirements for every nationality in your group early.
  • Insurance and medical: arrange travel and sports injury insurance, and keep a confidential list of allergies, medication and emergency contacts for each player.
  • Budget: set a clear budget for flights, the programme and spending money, and agree a payment timeline with parents or your club well in advance.
  • Communication: set up a team group for players and a separate one for parents, so updates reach the right people.
  • Roles: split jobs between staff, with one person on kit, one on medical and one on travel and timings.

Plan some downtime as well. A cultural experience and excursions are part of the programme, and a squad that enjoys the trip together usually plays better together.

How to register your team

Registration is done on the Global Elite 7s event page. The process is simple:

  • Choose whether you are registering as a team or as an individual player.
  • Enter your team name and team gender.
  • Add your city and country.
  • Select your team experience: club, academy, university, invitational, provincial or international.
  • Give the full name of the team manager, plus a WhatsApp number and email.
  • Send your details on WhatsApp or by email.

The organisers will then confirm eligibility and next steps. Because only top elite teams may register and there are 24 places for men and 24 for women, it makes sense to get your details in early and have your squad information ready for the follow-up.

If you have questions before you register, get in touch through the contact page, where you can choose Global Elite 7s as your topic. To add the dates to your diary or register now, visit the Global Elite 7s page.
