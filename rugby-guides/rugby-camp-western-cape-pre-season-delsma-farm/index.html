Why pre-season is the right time for a camp

Every school and club season is shaped long before the first fixture. A good rugby camp Western Cape players attend in the summer break gives them time to sharpen skills, learn the thinking behind a game plan and get to know teammates away from phones, traffic and the usual routine. Our pre-season camp at Delsma Farm is built around exactly that: skills, strategy and team bonding away from distractions.

The early weeks of the year are ideal. Players come back from the holidays a little rusty, and coaches often have only a handful of sessions before trials and selections. A focused block of training, good food and proper rest sets a standard that carries into the term. For younger players it is also a gentle first taste of living and training as a squad.

Camps suit players who are changing position, moving up an age group or coming back after a quiet year. Small details, such as passing off the weaker hand, body height in the tackle or setting up quickly at the breakdown, get far better attention when there is time to repeat them properly and talk them through.

Delsma Farm: a secluded venue with everything on site

Delsma Farm sits in a picturesque landscape of mountains and farmland, 12 km from the village of Riebeek-Kasteel. That distance helps. With no town bustle on the doorstep, the days stay simple: train, eat, rest and spend time with the group.

The venue has what a training camp needs in one place:

  • Field and equipment for skills work, unit practice and game-based sessions.
  • Gym for strength and conditioning. Young players should always train under supervision, with good technique coming well before heavy weights.
  • Swimming pool, handy for cooling down and light movement after hard sessions in the summer heat.
  • Meals are included, so players are fuelled properly without parents needing to plan anything.
  • Meeting room, a good space for tactics talks, planning and team discussions, where much of the strategy learning happens.
  • Accommodation on site, which is where friendships form and a group starts to feel like a team.

How players can get the most from five days

Five days goes quickly. Players who arrive prepared tend to take far more home with them. A few simple habits make a real difference:

  1. Arrive with a basic fitness base. A few weeks of running, skipping or ball work in December means the first sessions feel like training rather than survival.
  2. Set one or two personal goals. It might be a cleaner pass, a stronger tackle or better communication in defence. Clear goals make feedback easier to use.
  3. Drink water all day. January heat in the Western Cape is no joke. Sip steadily rather than waiting until thirst kicks in.
  4. Eat every meal. Growing players training hard need proper fuel, even when they do not feel hungry after a session.
  5. Sleep early. Late-night chats are part of camp, but rest is when the body adapts to training.
  6. Ask questions. Players who ask why a drill is done a certain way usually understand the game plan sooner.
  7. Mix with new faces. Training with players from other schools and clubs builds confidence and shows different ways of reading the game.

Small knocks and stiffness are normal at any camp. Encourage players to speak up early if something hurts, rather than training through it.

What to pack for camp

Players need to bring their own sleeping bag and towels, plus rugby kit. Beyond that, a sensible packing list looks like this:

  • Boots, ideally a pair suited to firm summer ground, plus trainers for the gym
  • Mouthguard and any protection the player normally wears
  • Several sets of training kit, as clothes rarely dry fully between sessions
  • A refillable water bottle, clearly named
  • Sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses for time outside
  • Swimwear and slip-slops for the pool
  • A warm top for cooler evenings in the countryside
  • Toiletries, any personal medication and a small first aid basics kit
  • A notebook and pen to jot down drills, feedback and goals

Label everything. With a group of teenagers sharing space, kit goes missing easily. If anything needs replacing before January, our rugby store stocks balls, protection and training gear.

Advice for parents and coaches

For parents: share any medical needs, allergies or dietary requirements when you book, and make sure your child knows how and when to take any medication. For younger players, especially those spending their first nights away, talk through what the days will look like. Knowing the routine of training, meals and rest takes much of the worry away. A short, upbeat goodbye usually helps more than a long one.

For coaches: a camp is a strong start to the season, particularly if a few players from the same squad attend together. Chat to players about what they want to work on beforehand, and follow up in the first weeks of term so the lessons from camp carry into team training. Players who return with new skills and confidence can help lift the whole group.
